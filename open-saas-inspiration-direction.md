# Open SaaS-Inspired Direction for FEC Playbook™

## Recommendation

Use the attached **Open SaaS** template as an **information-design reference**, not as a full visual reskin. Its strongest idea is a calmer, modular narrative: one focused hero, a visually led proof lane, a small number of editorial spotlights, then a structured capability grid. FEC Playbook™ should retain its existing bold sports/action system—true black, white, FEC cyan, angular transitions, oversized Montserrat headlines, and the play-button geometry—while borrowing the template’s breathing room and repeatable component rhythm.

> The resulting website would feel like an FEC operating playbook with the editorial clarity of a modern SaaS product—not a generic SaaS landing page and not a dense product manual.

## What the Attached Template Contributes

| Template pattern | What it does well | FEC Playbook™ translation |
|---|---|---|
| Centered hero plus a large product visual | Establishes one product story before detail | Keep FEC Playbook’s stronger left-biased action hero, but use a larger, cleaner evidence frame showing the Revenue Cycle or a real workflow screen. |
| Example carousel | Creates a moving proof lane instead of a static logo wall | Show real, labeled playbook examples: Inquiry Follow-Up, Party Booking, Visit Recovery, Reputation, and Repeat Visit. |
| Editorial two-column spotlights | Gives one key proof object room to matter | Use for **The Revenue System Is Built** and **From Revenue Review to Activation**. Copy on one side; a branded activation worksheet or product evidence frame on the other. |
| Variable-size bento grid | Organizes capabilities into an easy scan | Use a restrained, asymmetric **8 Revenue Playbooks** map—not a generic feature grid. Large tiles should represent revenue moments; smaller tiles can show the associated modules. |
| Light/dark token system | Makes supporting content feel clean and structured | Use light surfaces intentionally as editorial resets between dark campaign sections. Preserve the current dark-first public theme rather than adding a public theme switch. |

## Key Section Translations

### 1. Replace a Static Proof Strip with **Playbooks in Motion**

This is the most valuable template-derived addition. Directly after the hero, add an editorial light section with a horizontally scrollable proof lane. Each card should include a real screenshot or a clearly labeled diagram, a playbook name, and one outcome statement.

| Card | Suggested headline | Supporting line |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | **Inquiry Follow-Up** | Every lead receives the right next action before the opportunity cools. |
| 02 | **Party Booking** | Keep the booking journey moving from interest through confirmation. |
| 03 | **Visit Recovery** | Follow up while the guest experience is still top of mind. |
| 04 | **Reputation** | Route guest feedback into a repeatable response process. |
| 05 | **Repeat Visit** | Turn completed visits into the next reason to return. |

The visual treatment should use crisp black or white browser frames with a cyan active state, not ambient neon. On mobile, it should be manually swipeable and snap between cards. Any automatic progression should pause on interaction and respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

### 2. Recast the Template’s Bento Grid as an **8 Revenue Playbooks Map**

The template’s variable card sizing can improve the current page if it expresses hierarchy rather than adding more boxes. Use two large cards for the core booking and repeat-visit stories, four medium cards for the operational revenue moments, and two compact cards for supporting activation. Each card must remain fully readable without hover.

The play-button mark should act as the recurring graphic device: a corner notch, directional glyph, or cyan forward marker—not decorative glow. On hover or keyboard focus, a tile can reveal a short “What arrives built” line, but the baseline story must remain visible.

### 3. Borrow the Editorial Spotlight, Not the Template’s Centered Hero

FEC Playbook’s current hero is already more differentiated and conversion-ready than the template’s centered generic SaaS hero. Preserve it. Use the template’s two-column spotlight pattern farther down the page for the pre-built system promise:

> **YOU BRING THE BRAND. WE PROVIDE THE PLAYBOOK.**
>
> The automation logic, handoffs, and activation path arrive ready. FEC Playbook™ adapts the system to your facility, team, and existing software—then supports the work of putting it into operation.

The right-hand evidence object should be a branded activation worksheet, annotated workflow screen, or data-map view. It should never be a fabricated customer dashboard or generic tech artwork.

## Visual System: Keep vs. Change

| Keep exactly as FEC Playbook™ | Adapt from the template | Do not import |
|---|---|---|
| Black/white/cyan campaign palette | More breathing room between major proof moments | Template’s amber/purple gradients |
| Large, heavy, action-oriented typography | Editorial white sections as deliberate visual resets | Centered generic SaaS hero copy |
| Angular cuts, numbered sections, play-mark motif | Horizontally swipeable proof lane with visible labels | Dense equal-weight card grids |
| One primary Revenue Review CTA | Reusable section-title and feature-data patterns | Autoplay motion that hides content or interrupts reading |
| Real product or workflow proof | Optional bento hierarchy for the eight playbooks | Generic “used by” logos unless they are approved partners or customers |

## Mobile Behavior

The page should feel more editorial rather than merely smaller. The proof lane becomes full-bleed horizontal cards with visible partial next-card affordance. The 8-playbook map becomes an ordered vertical path rather than a compressed bento grid. White sections should not introduce pale gray text, and all cyan buttons must retain dark text or sufficient contrast.

## Recommended Decision

**Adopt the hybrid direction.** It gives FEC Playbook™ the template’s most valuable quality—modular, easy-to-scan product storytelling—while retaining the current conversion system and ownable sports/action identity.

The first implementation should be limited to two page-level changes: add **Playbooks in Motion** beneath the hero and reshape the existing eight-playbook presentation into the asymmetric map described above. This is enough to make the inspiration visibly clear without resetting the whole marketing site.

## Before Building

The proof lane needs five real or approved visual artifacts. The best inputs are anonymized workflow screenshots, branded workflow diagrams, or product screens that accurately represent each playbook. Do not use invented customer results, logos, facility names, or dashboards.
